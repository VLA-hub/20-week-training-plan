20-week running plan

Distances are miles. Tuesday and Thursday are easy, conversational runs; Saturday is the long run.

Week 1

BUILD
Run smart: Walk briskly for 5 minutes before and after. Use run/walk intervals and shorten sessions if needed. Repeat a week if recovery is poor.

Long-run guide

Start slower than your normal easy pace; walk breaks are fine. For runs over 60–90 minutes, practice roughly 30–60 g of carbohydrates per hour and drink according to thirst, weather and sweat rate.

Three-day strength split

Choose loads that leave 2–3 good reps in reserve. Rest 2–3 minutes for big lifts and 60–90 seconds for accessories.

Monday — Upper Body A

Wednesday — Lower Body

If Saturday's long run suffers, reduce squat and lunge sets first. Keep leg training moderate in the final eight weeks.

Friday — Upper Body B + Core

Progressive overload

Double progression: Build reps inside the range first. When all sets reach the top of the range with good form, add a small amount of weight and build reps again.

Typical increases: 2.5–5 lb for upper body and 5–10 lb for lower body, depending on the exercise. Hold steady if technique or recovery slips.

Important reminders

  • Easy runs should feel easy; you should be able to speak in full sentences.
  • Sharp, worsening or localized pain—or pain that changes your stride—is a reason to stop and reassess.
  • Week 17's 11-mile run is a target, not a requirement. You do not need to run 13.1 miles in training.
  • Weeks 19–20 taper. Avoid hard lower-body lifting in race week.
